Part of writing these reviews is an attempt to make the things I read stick.

I have felt the thrill of recieving an electrifying thought, having my brain swim with the implications, light up connections to a range ideas.

This education, however, can be fleeting.
Looking back over the things I've read, how much remains? How many lines might be discarded in the way to a point? But sometimes the point, too is lost.

There are things I know I liked when I read, saw or listened to them, but couldn't now tell you what or why.

Instead I find, a vibe, left. An emotional imprint. I like that, but I can no longer clearly tell you why.

It didn't stick.
But, at the time it was deeply felt.

I believe the diagnosis of Hyperpolitics to be a similar effect - our politics erupts into emotive, explosive outbursts. Yet lacks any staying power. Noting collates, just a series of events without a momentum for change.

The cause, too, resonates. I rarely need to see others if I don't actively want to. Sometimes I also need to kick against gravity to find that want. I have club or coffeehouse to forment debate, no village hall nor chapel to hear preach, no union that structures my livelihood and life, no institutional focus to cultivate and grow an idea into a politics.

Instead I have a workplace, that despite its agency, is no democracy. A local and national politics that asks nothing of me except periodic attention, widespread inattention and a vote once every so many years.

Like the books, the ideas are private, privately considered. They are not rehursed, not exercised on the unconvinced.

I once hoped the internet would provide a venue. But that optimism is long past exhausted. It is easy to come to, easy to go. Little is invested, and so little ventured. Where things may be sincerely felt but there is little incentive to sit in discomfort or change a mind - we just move on.

I write these things as a partial remedy. I expect no connection no common cause, I neither know nor care if anyone reads this. But there is a discipline or rehearsal that matters to me - in the hopes I will retain more until I get a chance to discuss an idea with someone else.

I think the point of Jägers book, is that politically the venues for that diminish, and explain a lot about our emotionally unstable, stolid and unretentive world.
