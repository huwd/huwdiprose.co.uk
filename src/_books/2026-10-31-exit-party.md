I really enjoyed Station 11, I bought Exit Party soon after finishing it.

And then... I didn't start it, it kept slipping down my list until a chat with a friend bumped it back up.

I heard a contemporary US civil war was an element and expected it to be the whole. Feared another example of folks working through the political polarisation of the current '20s. Perhaps mostly, feared it'd be a crass "wake up people", line. Trying to shock folks out of complacency. "Imagine it can happen here".

Ten years ago [Save the Children did an advert](https://youtu.be/RBQ-IoHfimQ) which mobilised the idea to great and shocking effect. Though there to build empathy for elsewhere rather than real fear for "here". I still think about it.

Basically I worried the book would be a tedious lecture.

I need not have worried. The backdrop is there, and there are things to be said about order and disorder, as well as dislocation, being a refugee. However there's far more about who we are, and might be.

The characters become real, their decisions have fidelity. They may surprise but they're faithful to the people described.

A strong theme is the question of what is intrinsic or incidental in choices we make? What's in a person, what's environmental? The results are stark because we choose some fairly dramatic environments.

I enjoyed it, even the odd, slightly disjointed structure. I'd recommend it to many, especially if you liked Station 11.
