I found the introduction stark and a little off-puttingly polemic.
I thought I'd bounce off it, but it builds a decent argument around its claims and justifies its ire as it goes on.

It sits well alongside other things I've read recently, most of all Quinn Slobodian's Crack-Up Capitalism, but also his [Hayek's Bastards](/review/book/2025/12/23/hayeks-bastards/) and [Muskism](/review/book/2026/05/01/muskism/) with Ben Tarnoff, and Karen Hao's [Empire of AI](/review/book/2025/12/05/empire-of-ai/). Together they give a good view into Silicon Valley.

This one is fairly specifically focused, biographically, on Peter Thiel, the [PayPal Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal_Mafia) and his wider network of fellow travellers. It makes the case that there's a coherent ideology here though.
One that has little time for democracy, believes it knows better than voters. It believes in the natural superiority of some people, apparently emerging out of essential characteristics, not just learned behaviour. A "cognitive elite". It believes these people should be elevated, that the world would be better run by them, in structures modelled on corporations in familiar C-suite patterns. In Curtis Yarvin's words, "a national CEO is what's called a dictator".

It makes a bogeyman out of a state that could limit the freedoms of these men to do as they will, while drawing significant revenue from state contracts. It seeks to crack up the global order, creating new jurisdictions and spaces to operate in.

It has a faith in technology to bring this all about, viewing it as a tool to ideally replace but practically suppress and influence politics.

It sees an organised, recently heavily empowered and activist vanguard, made up mostly of incredibly wealthy tech investors and CEOs: nerds.

To me, this amounts to a project that cannot be seen as anything other than extremely right wing, especially by European standards.

The book does a good job of presenting this as a description that seems historically faithful, technically accurate and appropriately resonant to earn the label fascist.
