Starting as a history of bonds it's a good romp through what can be done with good finance.

At a certain point it turns into a modern era and gets more and more technical, still told through select characters. I enjoyed those bits slightly less, but perhaps that's just trying to keep up amidst the proliferation of financial jargon.

The book does a good job of making it accessible though. It makes me want to go back and read Adam Tooze's Crashed. A book I enjoyed at the time but find I've retained very little of.

By its conclusion, the warning is there, but it's hard to feel it weighs much against the affection. I fear it's weirdly into the whole thing. Like a scientist studying ants, giddy with the complexity of what has been found, who is now unable to be annoyed if they invade their kitchen.

You could come away with a rather pleasant view of the whole thing, despite the messes described.

What I got, is that sometimes you need to do things so big, that you can only borrow from the future. Fixed Income products (Bonds & co) are a good financial technology to enable that. Finance _can_ create something here with a real world economic impact (or intended harm).

We meet iterations of this along the way, and I can see where futures, and swaps, overnight lending and money markets become similar lubricant for a real world economy. Hedging and short selling help to manage risk and enable things.

But there's also a large element of this which just seems like a respectable version of a betting shop flutter. At scale. With (unintended?) harms if it goes terribly wrong. Something on my mind as we see the regulatory tussle over the new emergence of prediction markets in the USA. Is it just sports betting? or an iteration on futures? or something else?

This book is very interested in the technology, and the impressive things it's wrought. However it seems less interested in the question of how (or if?) you can ensure it stays harnessed to a real economy and not merely a route to power and wealth, or a giant roulette wheel where others pay off bad bets.
